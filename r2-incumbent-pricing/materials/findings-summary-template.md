alongside the appended rows in `starter-benchmarks.md`. Short is fine — a few sentences per section.

## 1. What each vertical costs today
One short paragraph per vertical: the typical unit and the price band you actually found, and how
much confidence the band deserves given how many rows support it. Give the band, not a single
number, and say which end is thinly sourced.

## 2. Where the money concentrates
Which of your verticals carries the highest spend per outcome, and which looks most compressible —
i.e. where the incumbent price seems least connected to the underlying cost of delivering it? This
is the question the table exists to answer, so back the claim with specific rows.

## 3. Where the price war already happened
Any vertical where prices are visibly converging toward a floor, undercut by a free or bundled tier,
or where vendors compete on published price at all. Entering one of these is a different game than
entering a "contact sales" market.

## 4. What you couldn't price, and why
List every vendor or sub-segment where you found **no** usable number. For each: what you tried, and
what would unblock it (a quote request, a specific procurement portal, a trade survey behind a
paywall). Be specific — this section is what stops the next person repeating your dead ends, and a
well-documented "no published price anywhere in this category" is a real finding, not a gap.

## 5. Anything that surprised you
Assumptions the numbers contradicted, pricing structures you didn't expect, vendors that turned out
not to be competitors at all. Optional, but usually the most valuable section.

## 6. Notes for the next contributor
Sources that paid off and sources that wasted your time; search phrasings that worked; anything
about the format or these instructions that was unclear or wrong.
